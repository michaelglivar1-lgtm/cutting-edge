The Contractor Who Builds His Own Software

How Michael Glivar is bringing operational intelligence into the business of building.

By Michael Glivar
Vice President, Cutting Edge Design & Construction, Inc.
November 3, 2026

Most general contractors buy their software the way they buy their trucks: off a lot, built by someone else, designed for an average operator in an average market. Michael Glivar took a different road. Frustrated by tools that recorded his business without ever improving it, the Vice President of Cutting Edge Design & Construction, Inc. sat down and invented his own. The result is PICKET — Michael Glivar’s patent-pending operational-intelligence system for construction, supported by two companion systems: SKYNET — the AI building network, which holds context, priorities, and forecasts, and Trinity, which governs policy and decisions. Together they form an intelligence layer supporting how the company finds and evaluates its work. Cutting Edge operates under Florida Certified General Contractor license CGC060801, held by its qualifying contractor, Michael Lee Gillespie.

The premise behind PICKET is that construction opportunities announce themselves early and in public. A hotel approaching a brand-mandated renovation deadline, a condominium association working toward a milestone inspection, a county posting a seawall replacement — each generates records long before it generates a request for proposals that lands in a contractor’s inbox. The challenge has never been secrecy. It has been attention. No estimating department can watch hundreds of public sources across multiple counties and states, every day, without missing the one posting that mattered.

PICKET brings those signals together. The system collects public data and builds ranked commercial queues: opportunities sorted by fit, timing, and value, presented to Glivar’s team in the order they deserve attention. The aim is for estimators to start the morning with a prioritized picture of the market instead of a blank search bar. Monitoring can continue beyond office hours, while the team checks source records, deadlines, and bid requirements before acting.

From public signals to live pursuits

The system is being applied in three arenas. In hospitality, PICKET tracks the signals that precede hotel renovation work — ownership changes, brand requirements, and property conditions — and has been used on live hotel pursuits, where renovation deadlines set by the brands create urgency that rewards the contractor who arrives early and prepared.

In the public arena, it supports the bid pipeline that, in November 2026, has Cutting Edge pursuing three Volusia County projects: the Seaview Vehicular Beach Access Ramp Reconstruction (26-B-128CW), the River Beach Drive Vehicular Beach Access Ramp Reconstruction (26-B-129CW), and the Daytona Beach Ocean Center Kitchen Renovation (26-P-126JD). The ramp projects include seawall work. These are active pursuits, not claims of awarded contracts.

And in storm response — the arena where speed matters most — the team applied the system during the severe flooding that struck Brevard County and nearby areas on November 1–2, 2026. As rainfall totals climbed past a foot in parts of the region, the team identified affected corridors and properties and began outreach to owners with offers of damage assessment and repair. Glivar’s objective is to shorten the gap between public information and useful action on the ground.

A learning loop built around the work

What Glivar wants to separate PICKET from a monitoring tool is the learning loop. The system is designed to record pursuits with their outcomes. A won bid, a passed opportunity, a lost competition — each can become a data point for evaluating the next ranking. Over time, the goal is to develop an accumulating, evidence-based sense of which opportunities fit this company, in these markets, at these values. Glivar describes it as operational intelligence that keeps working. Its usefulness ultimately depends on recorded outcomes, reliable source data, and the team’s review.

There is a structural reason this inventor is a contractor rather than a software founder who wandered into construction. Glivar develops as well as builds. Through Brothers 59 Construction LLC, the company he co-owns with his wife Lucia and operates in Colorado and Florida, he takes principal positions and develops at cost. Building below market basis is an advantage a builder-developer can pursue by controlling delivery costs. PICKET was designed by someone who carries both sides of that risk, and it shows in what the system is intended to optimize for: pursuits worth a builder’s balance sheet.

The broader significance is easy to state and hard to imitate. Every industry has a small number of operators who stop renting the tools of their trade and start inventing them. Those operators can compound their advantages: better information produces better pursuits, better pursuits produce better outcomes, and better outcomes inform the next decision. Glivar’s wager is that the construction company of the next decade is an intelligence company with a contracting license — one whose software learns from the work while its crews deliver it. In Florida, he is putting that idea to work. He simply decided to build his tools first.
