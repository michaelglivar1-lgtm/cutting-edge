Executing the PIP Without Killing RevPAR

A conversion or re-flag lives or dies on two documents: the executed franchise agreement and the Property Improvement Plan attached to it. The PIP defines every line item the franchisor requires to bring the asset onto the new flag — from soft goods and case goods to signage, technology infrastructure, ADA compliance, and life-safety upgrades — along with the completion deadlines that trigger default clauses if missed.

Cutting Edge Design & Construction manages the PIP as a controlled scope, not a wish list. We reconcile the franchisor's PIP against the actual field conditions during pre-construction, flag discrepancies before they become change orders, and build a submittal log tied to brand-approved product lines (Marriott's MODA and GreenLeaf programs, Hilton's brand-approved vendor matrices, IHG's Formule and design intent packages, Hyatt and Wyndham equivalents). Guest-impact is engineered into the schedule from day one: we phase by floor or stack, maintain sellable inventory through the work, and sequence public-space and back-of-house scopes around occupancy patterns so the property keeps generating revenue while it converts.

Phasing, Guest-Impact, and the Conversion Deadline

Most conversions run against a hard franchisor clock — commonly 12 to 24 months from agreement execution to full brand compliance, with interim milestones for signage, guestroom completion percentages, and public areas. Blowing a milestone can mean liquidated damages, delayed key-money disbursement, or loss of the flag. We build a phasing plan backward from those dates.

  • Guestroom phasing: Renovate in vertical stacks or wings to protect ADR and keep a saleable block online. We coordinate with the property's rooms and revenue teams so displacement matches shoulder-season and low-occupancy windows.
  • Public-space sequencing: Lobby, breakfast/F&B, fitness, and pool areas are scoped to minimize guest-facing disruption — temporary layouts, off-hours work, and dust/noise containment that meets brand cleanliness and safety expectations.
  • Long-lead FF&E control: Brand-approved case goods, soft goods, and signage carry 8–16 week lead times. We place procurement early against approved cut sheets so material arrival never becomes the critical-path failure.
  • Life-safety and infrastructure: Sprinkler, fire alarm, electrical, and low-voltage/Wi-Fi upgrades are integrated into the phasing so inspections and brand audits pass on the first walk.

Brand Compliance and the Final Franchisor Audit

A conversion isn't finished when construction is; it's finished when the franchisor's quality-assurance or design-review team signs off. Cutting Edge Design & Construction builds toward that audit from the start — documenting brand-approved finishes, maintaining a compliance binder of submittals and product approvals, and running an internal pre-audit walk that mirrors the franchisor's checklist. We coordinate directly with brand design managers and franchise-services representatives so interpretation questions get resolved in writing, not discovered on the final walk. Our closeout package gives owners and asset managers the documentation trail lenders, franchisors, and future PIP cycles will ask for.
