Cost Per Key by Scope of Work

PIP (Property Improvement Plan) budgets vary widely because "renovation" spans everything from a case-goods swap to a full flag change. The ranges below reflect 2026 material, labor, and freight conditions across the Southeast and Eastern Seaboard corridors we serve, and assume a select-service to upper-midscale asset. Full-service and luxury tiers trend 30–60% higher due to specification and back-of-house complexity.

  • Soft goods only — $8,000–$18,000/key: Carpet/LVT, window treatments, bedding, lampshades, wall vinyl, paint, and artwork. Lowest guest-impact, shortest downtime. Typical PIP cycle item at the 5–7 year mark.
  • Soft goods + case goods — $18,000–$35,000/key: Adds headboards, casegoods (dressers, desks, nightstands), seating, and lighting fixtures. Often triggered by brand refresh mandates and aging FF&E.
  • Full renovation — $45,000–$95,000/key: Guestroom gut including bath vanities, tub-to-shower conversions, drywall, MEP touches, corridors, lobby, F&B, and amenity spaces. This is the bulk of mid-cycle PIP activity.
  • Brand conversion — $85,000–$150,000+/key: Full de-flag/re-flag: exterior, signage, lobby reconfiguration, brand-mandated amenities (grab-and-go, fitness, market), room count/layout changes, and life-safety upgrades to current code. Highest cost and longest timeline.

These figures exclude soft costs (design, procurement fees, permits, FF&E freight and warehousing) that typically add 12–20% to the hard-cost line, as well as any deferred-maintenance or code-driven scope uncovered during due diligence.

Phasing to Protect Occupancy and Guest Experience

The largest hidden cost in any PIP is displaced revenue. We build renovation schedules around your occupancy patterns — typically renovating in floor-by-floor or wing-by-wing "pods" of 20–40 rooms so the property stays open and revenue-generating throughout. Corridor, elevator lobby, and public-space work is sequenced to minimize guest-facing disruption, with hard-wall dust partitions, dedicated service elevators, and off-peak demolition windows.

For brand conversions with a hard franchise deadline, we sequence life-safety and brand-critical items first to protect the flag, then absorb finish work into a rolling schedule. Owners and asset managers see a per-pod draw schedule and a rooms-out-of-order (OOO) forecast up front, so displaced-revenue impact is modeled — not discovered.

PIP Compliance and Brand Standards

A PIP is a contractual document, not a wish list. Cutting Edge Design & Construction reads the PIP line by line against current brand design standards — Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Hyatt, Wyndham, and Choice — and flags any item where the PIP language and the latest brand spec diverge before we price it. That prevents the common failure mode where an owner pays for a finished product that the brand inspector rejects at reopening.

We coordinate directly with brand construction managers and design review, submit finish and FF&E specifications for approval, and document compliance so the property clears its final PIP inspection on the first walk. When brand standards change mid-project, we manage the change order and re-approval rather than absorbing a punch-list surprise at the finish line.

FAQ

How accurate is a per-key estimate before design is complete?
A per-key range gets an owner to a go/no-go decision, but Cutting Edge Design & Construction converts it to a fixed or GMP number only after we've reviewed the actual PIP document, walked the asset, and confirmed brand spec. Expect the early range to tighten by ±10% once scope is locked.

Can we stay open during a full renovation?
Yes. Most of our full renovations run with the hotel operating at reduced inventory. Cutting Edge Design & Construction phases work in pods and gives you an OOO forecast so you can manage displaced demand and rate strategy alongside the schedule.

Who handles FF&E procurement — us or you?
Either. Cutting Edge Design & Construction works with owner-furnished FF&E and with brand-approved procurement firms, and we can manage freight, warehousing, and installation logistics. We coordinate delivery windows to the phasing plan so casegoods don't sit in a parking lot accruing storage fees.

What most often blows the PIP budget?
Deferred maintenance and code triggers uncovered behind walls — MEP, ADA, and life-safety items a brand conversion forces to current code. Cutting Edge Design & Construction front-loads due diligence to surface these before contract, so contingency is a line item, not a mid-project emergency.
