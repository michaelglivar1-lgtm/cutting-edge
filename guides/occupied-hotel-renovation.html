Phasing That Protects Sellable Inventory and Guest Scores

The core constraint of an occupied renovation is simple: every room you take offline is revenue you can't recover, and every guest who hears a hammer at 8 a.m. is a review you can't unwrite. Cutting Edge Design & Construction builds the phasing plan around your booking curve, not our crew's convenience.

We typically renovate in wings or floor "stacks" of 15–30 keys per phase, sized to your occupancy forecast so displacement stays inside natural low-demand windows and revenue management still has rooms to sell at rate. Practical controls we deploy on every occupied job:

  • Buffer floors and hard barriers — an unsold floor or a sealed, negative-pressure containment zone between active work and occupied rooms.
  • Noise-restricted work hours for demo, core drilling, and anything above ~70 dB, scheduled around checkout/check-in and confirmed with the front desk daily.
  • Dedicated service routes — separate freight elevator, back-of-house corridors, and staging so guests never share a path with dust, debris, or crews.
  • Daily "make it presentable" standard — corridors, elevators, and lobby touchpoints returned to guest-ready condition before peak arrival each evening.

The goal is that a guest three doors from an active room can't tell a renovation is happening. That's what keeps cleanliness and quietness scores intact through the project.

Brand Standards, PIP Compliance, and QA Sign-Off

Most occupied renovations are PIP-driven — a Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Hyatt, Wyndham, or Choice property improvement plan with a hard compliance deadline attached to your franchise agreement. Cutting Edge Design & Construction reads the PIP as a scope document and a schedule risk at the same time.

We build the phase sequence backward from your PIP deadline so the last key is turned before the compliance date, not after. We hold FF&E to approved brand specs and lead times, coordinate mock-room approvals with your brand's design and QA representatives before we run the full floor, and document each phase for brand sign-off so there are no surprises at the final inspection. When long-lead FF&E (casegoods, soft goods, lighting packages) drives the critical path, we phase construction so trades stay productive while product ships — rather than idling the whole property waiting on a container.

Cost, Schedule, and Guest-Impact Trade-Offs

Phased work costs more per key than a full closure — you're paying for repeated mobilization, containment, off-hours labor, and slower production. But a full closure means zero revenue for the entire duration plus ramp-up cost to rebuild occupancy and rate afterward. For most limited- and select-service and mid-scale full-service assets on the Eastern Seaboard, staying open at reduced inventory beats going dark. We model both scenarios against your P&L and RevPAR so ownership decides with real numbers, not a gut call. Where a short, targeted closure of a single amenity (pool, fitness, a food outlet) actually accelerates the schedule and reduces total guest impact, we'll recommend it — and time it to your softest demand period.
